Predicted stress came from results\stress\stress_scores_on_deception.csv (one fixed score per clip; high/low split at each dataset's truthful-clip median: bol 0.057, rlt 0.751, so the bands are relative). Predicted stress vs the deception label (per clip; point-biserial r with p, Mann-Whitney U p, and the AUC of stress as a deception score): bol r = +0.20 (p < 0.001), Mann-Whitney p = 0.250, AUC 0.54; rlt r = +0.03 (p = 0.763), Mann-Whitney p = 0.944, AUC 0.50. For the plain two-head model, false positives among truthful test predictions were, per dataset, bol low 121/246 (49.2%) vs high 133/243 (54.7%) and rlt low 29/90 (32.2%) vs high 28/90 (31.1%); pooled over both, the FPR is not reliably different between bands (difference +3.7 points, 95% CI -10.7 to +19.6, which includes 0). For the stress-conditioned model the counts were bol low 92/246 (37.4%) vs high 119/243 (49.0%) and rlt low 31/90 (34.4%) vs high 21/90 (23.3%); pooled, the FPR is not reliably different between bands (difference +5.4 points, 95% CI -9.4 to +21.3, which includes 0). So the data do not show a reliable concentration of false alarms among stressed truthful speakers. Run by run on the high-stress truthful band (same folds and seeds), the conditioned model's FPR minus the plain model's was -5.1 +/- 16.3 points over 15 pooled runs (lower in 5, higher in 4, equal in 6; Wilcoxon p = 0.314); per dataset: bol -5.1 points (p = 0.314), rlt -6.5 points (p = 0.144). Caveats: clips of the same person and the three seeds are not independent (hence the group bootstrap), the stress score is itself a noisy MAS-trained model applied to other datasets, and the bands are small (bol 81 and rlt 30 high-stress truthful clips).
